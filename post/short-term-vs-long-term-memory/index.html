Understanding Short Term vs Long Term Memory

October 1, 2026 · Vicenta L. Hensley

Understanding the differences between short term and long term memory is crucial for anyone studying psychology. Short term memory holds information temporarily, while long term memory stores it more permanently. Recognizing these distinctions will help you develop effective strategies for learning and retention.

What is short term memory?

Short term memory, also known as working memory, is the ability to hold a small amount of information for a brief period. Typically, it can retain about seven items for around 15 to 30 seconds. For example, when you try to remember a phone number long enough to dial it, you are using your short term memory. Once you finish dialing, that number often fades from your mind unless it is transferred to long term memory.

What is long term memory?

Long term memory refers to the storage of information over extended periods, potentially for a lifetime. Unlike short term memory, it has a much larger capacity and can retain information for days, months, or even years. It can store complex information, including skills, experiences, and facts. For instance, learning a language or mastering a musical instrument involves accessing long term memory, as these skills require practice and repetition to embed.

How do short term and long term memory differ?

Short term memory is fleeting and limited in capacity, while long term memory is expansive and can store information indefinitely. Retrieval also differs; short term memory allows for immediate access, whereas long term memory often requires cues or context for recall.

Why are both types of memory important?

Both short term and long term memory play vital roles in cognitive processes and learning. Short term memory allows you to actively process and manipulate information, which is essential for tasks like problem-solving or decision-making. Long term memory is crucial for retaining knowledge and skills necessary for academic success and personal development. Together, they facilitate effective learning, enabling you to absorb new information and integrate it with what you already know.

Common misconceptions about memory types

Many people confuse short term memory with long term memory, assuming they serve the same purpose. Another common misconception is that short term memory can be significantly expanded through practice, while it has inherent limitations. Additionally, some believe that once information is in long term memory, it is permanent and cannot be forgotten. In reality, memories can fade or be altered over time.

Which memory type should you choose?

When deciding which memory type to focus on, consider your goals. If you need to retain information for immediate tasks, such as studying for a test, prioritize strategies that enhance short term memory, like rehearsal or mnemonic devices. For long-term retention of knowledge and skills, engage in practices that facilitate transfer to long term memory, such as spaced repetition and meaningful associations.

Conclusion

To enhance your learning, consider how to effectively use both short term and long term memory. Techniques like repetition and organization can strengthen your long term memory. Recognizing when to rely on short term memory for immediate tasks can also improve your cognitive efficiency.
