Understanding RAG Answers: Why They Cite Sources but Contradict Them

October 1, 2026 · MachineryHacks · Vicenta L. Hensley

RAG answers use a Red-Amber-Green color-coding system to indicate project status, helping project managers quickly convey progress and risks. However, it’s common to encounter RAG answers that cite a source while contradicting it, which can create confusion for stakeholders.

What exactly is a RAG answer?

A RAG answer is a visual tool in project management that shows the status of a project or task at a glance. The colors represent different levels of risk or progress: Red signifies critical issues, Amber indicates caution or potential problems, and Green means everything is on track. These answers are included in status reports to provide stakeholders with immediate insight into a project's condition and to identify areas needing attention.

Why might a RAG answer cite a source but contradict it?

Several factors can lead to a RAG answer referencing a source while contradicting it. One common issue is the misinterpretation of data. If a project manager misreads a report, they might summarize the status incorrectly even while citing data that suggests a different conclusion.

Changes in project status can also cause contradictions. If a project's situation shifts after the source was created, the RAG answer may not reflect this new reality. Human error plays a significant role; inaccuracies in data entry or communication can introduce conflicting information.

Additionally, different stakeholders may interpret the same source variably based on their perspectives or priorities, leading to inconsistencies in reporting.

How can I verify the information in a RAG answer?

To verify the information in a RAG answer, follow these steps:

  1. Check the source cited in the RAG answer for its context and relevance.
  2. Cross-reference the data with other reports or updates from the project to see if it aligns.
  3. Look for any recent communications or updates that might indicate changes in status since the source was created.
  4. If possible, discuss the matter with the project manager or team member responsible for the RAG answer to clarify any discrepancies.

What should I do if I find contradictions in RAG answers?

When you encounter contradictions in RAG answers, address them promptly. Start by gathering all relevant information, including the source of the RAG answer and any conflicting data. Then, reach out to the person responsible for the report to discuss your findings and seek clarification. If necessary, escalate the issue to higher management to ensure that stakeholders receive accurate updates. Document the discrepancies and any resolutions reached to maintain a clear record.

Are there common misconceptions about RAG reporting?

One misconception is that a Red status always indicates project failure. In reality, it may point to significant issues that can still be resolved with effective management. Another misunderstanding is that the colors are fixed; they can change based on new data or stakeholder input. Additionally, some believe that RAG answers are purely quantitative, but qualitative assessments can also influence the color coding.

Conclusion

If you encounter RAG answers that seem contradictory, take the time to investigate the sources and context behind them. Understanding the nuances of project reporting will help you navigate these complexities and ensure you're working with accurate information.
